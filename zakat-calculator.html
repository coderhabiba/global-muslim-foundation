Knowledge Hub

Frequently Asked Questions on Zakat

Clear rulings based on standard consensus to help you calculate and fulfill with peace of heart.

Should I use Gold or Silver Nisab for my calculations?

expand_more
Classical scholars and majority contemporary fatwa councils recommend using the Silver Nisab for cash and liquid wealth. Because silver has a lower dollar threshold, it allows more destitute families to benefit from collective aid. Gold Nisab is commonly utilized when assessing gold bullion directly.

How do I calculate Zakat on pensions, 401(k), and retirement funds?

expand_more
If your retirement fund is inaccessible without heavy early-withdrawal penalties, Zakat is only due upon the actual accessible cash value or calculated on the underlying zakatable assets (cash and trade equities, roughly 25-40% of the total fund portfolio) after subtracting estimated exit tax penalties.

Is Zakat due on personal jewelry worn regularly?

expand_more
According to the Hanafi school of jurisprudence, Zakat is due on all gold and silver regardless of whether it is worn or stored. In the Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali schools, customary personal jewelry kept without intention of asset storage is exempt. For precaution, many donors choose to purify all precious metals.

Can Zakat be paid in advance for emergency crises like Gaza?

expand_more
Yes, it is permissible and highly encouraged according to authentic prophetic narrations to prepay Zakat one or two years ahead to alleviate acute disasters, famine, and medical crises facing vulnerable communities.
